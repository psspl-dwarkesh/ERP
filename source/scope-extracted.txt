1. Business and operating context
The customer’s published business information describes industrial automation/turnkey projects, electrical and instrumentation solutions, calibration, computerized system validation, manufacturing IT, annual maintenance contracts and operations & maintenance. It also references engineering design, commissioning, service teams and calibration activities. This supports an engineering-and-service-centric operating model rather than a purely distribution-focused ERP. Public website descriptions are contextual research, not evidence of implemented internal workflows.
Design implication: the ERP should preserve a single chain of reference from customer enquiry to quotation/order, project or production execution, procurement, material movement, site delivery, installed asset and recurring support; HR timesheets, payroll-related cost and external finance references should reconcile along that chain.

2. Objectives, delivery scope and boundaries
2.1 Business outcomes
Establish a consistent record of commercial, engineering, physical stock, field operations and people data.
Reduce duplicate entries and manual handoffs among sales, engineering, purchasing, site stores, HR and service.
Provide end-to-end traceability for BOM changes, purchase approvals, serial items, testing and site movement.
Enable project, panel, contract and technician accountability and management insight.
Support internal controls, audit evidence and privacy-aware permissions while allowing agreed accounting interoperability.
2.2 Included software delivery work
Business analysis, requirements workshops, FRD, workflow catalog, UI/UX specifications and approved wireframes.
Application frontend, backend/business services, relational data model, secured APIs, scheduled/background jobs and integration adaptors.
Configuration of masters, roles, approval rules, forms, reports, dashboards, document handling and notifications agreed in FRD.
Functional/unit/integration/system/security/performance/UAT support and defect remediation.
Environment build, production deployment, documentation, knowledge transfer, cutover and post-launch support under agreed terms.
2.3 Scope organizing principle
No new standalone modules are introduced beyond the eleven named modules. Panel manufacture is modeled as an Inventory/Project workflow; preventive/contract services are modeled through Asset/Project, with billing events passed to Sales. Shared authorization, documents, approvals, integrations, notifications and audit logging are foundational cross-module platform services.
2.4 Delivery definition
One production release comprising all eleven modules. Development and test can be organized into internal workstreams, but no individual module is deemed live/accepted before combined end-to-end testing, data reconciliation, security validation and business approval for the integrated release. Dates, warranty and support SLA remain unconfirmed.

3.2 Canonical business linkages
Customer / Lead -> Opportunity / RFQ -> Quotation -> Sales order / contract -> Project or sales fulfilment -> (BOM/MRP/PR/PO/GRN/QC/stock/assembly) -> Dispatch / commissioning -> Customer installed asset -> AMC/service ticket -> technician visit/spares -> recurring or chargeable billing event -> external accounting reconciliation. HR (attendance/deployment/timesheets/claims) contributes to labour allocation, project costing, service execution and payroll.
3.3 Core state and approval design principles
Each material document should have explicit draft/submitted/approved/rejected/cancelled/closed statuses suited to its life cycle; the final state model is an FRD deliverable.
After approval, sensitive changes require revision/change record or controlled reversal rather than undetectable overwrite.
All cross-module transactional handoffs carry origin ID, destination ID, outcome, time and error/retry state where asynchronous.
Entitlements, financial approvals, inventory valuation and payroll processing require independent authority rules.
Master IDs and cross-links should be generated consistently; no duplicate project/customer/material identities without controlled merge.

14.3 Key implementation risks and mitigations
Risk | Details / decisions
Overbroad module labels | Why it matters: Large functionality ambiguity across eleven modules.Mitigation / decision gate: Detailed FRD workshops with priority, workflow sign-off and traceability.
Accounting ownership | Why it matters: Duplicate or missing invoices/ledger postings.Mitigation / decision gate: Source-of-truth matrix and integration PoC before design freeze.
Complex multi-site stock | Why it matters: Stock and serialized equipment lose custody trace.Mitigation / decision gate: Pilot physical flows including site stores and subcontractors; reconcile count/value.
One-release integration risk | Why it matters: A defect in one core module can block overall go-live.Mitigation / decision gate: Early integration environments, interface contract tests and full dress-rehearsal UAT.
Payroll/statutory correctness | Why it matters: Legal/pay errors and sensitive data exposure.Mitigation / decision gate: Payroll policy approval, parallel pay runs and restricted review.
Manufacturing scope growth | Why it matters: BOM and routing complexity can shift architecture and effort.Mitigation / decision gate: Validate 2-3 representative panel products and revisions before estimation.
Undefined data quality | Why it matters: Opening balance and master data errors invalidate outputs.Mitigation / decision gate: Source profiling, ownership, reconciliation and cutover freeze.
Regulated evidence assumptions | Why it matters: Validation burden may differ substantially.Mitigation / decision gate: Early compliance impact assessment and explicit acceptance plan.
200+ distributed user performance | Why it matters: Concurrency/site connectivity can degrade experience.Mitigation / decision gate: Define and load-test realistic concurrent workload and field network conditions.
